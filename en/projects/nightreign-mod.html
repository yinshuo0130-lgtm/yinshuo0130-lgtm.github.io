ELDEN RING NIGHTREIGN
Content Expansion Mod

Built on the base game's framework, the mod adds 7 Nightlords, 12 Night Laws and the Lake of Rot shifting-earth terrain, reworks talisman growth, and supports seamless co-op for 1–3 players. The beta went public on 1 November 2026 with tester calls on Bilibili and Douyin, followed by two updates within three days based on player feedback.

01

Nightlords & Map

Two runs against the same Nightlord can bring different enemies on the first two days.

Seven new Nightlords: the Fire Giant, Bayle, Dragonlord Placidusax, Radahn, Maliketh, Malenia and Messmer. Each has two random sets of early-night bosses and their adds. Open-world bosses from the base game were turned into final bosses, with new health pools, second phases and multiplayer scaling for 1–3 players.

Every Nightlord has its own icon, battle music, sky atmosphere and Day 3 arena, matching the presentation standard of the base game. They can be picked directly in normal expeditions and also appear at random in Deep of Night.

Lake of Rot shifting earth

The Lake of Rot replaces the forest region and has a surface layer and an underground layer. On the surface, resonance crystals spawn at random across three zones, giving 64 combinations; only after all of them are destroyed does the seal break and the portal open to the underground passage. Portals link the two layers to cut down on dead running. The whole map holds 152 enemies and 44 loot spots, with a guardian and a Site of Grace at the deepest point underground.

The shifting earth takes over the base game's forest slot: when the engine rolls the lake layout, a native plugin swaps that run's forest terrain files for the lake before the run starts, and every other run keeps the forest. It can appear in normal expeditions and in Deep of Night at about 5%, on par with the other shifting-earth events.

02

Growth & Revives

In the base game, reviving a teammate only limits losses. The new revive buff gives the revived player a reason to fight back and makes reviving itself more rewarding.

Growth curves and three drop tiers were reworked for 30 talismans, so a talisman found on Day 1 is still a meaningful choice on Day 3. After being revived by a teammate, a player's attack power rises step by step.

Development took more than 80 rounds of in-game testing, most of it spent tracking down crashes. Every issue was traced to its root cause before it was fixed; nothing was cut to work around a problem.

03

Night Laws: one rule per run

Night Laws are rule cards, one per run, that affect routing, how resources are spent, timing and teamwork. Every law has an upside and a cost, and none of them simply makes enemies stronger. All 12 are implemented and shipped with the beta, with values tuned separately for 1, 2 and 3 players.

The 12 laws follow four design principles:

  • Principle 1The upside and the cost act on the same decisionBurning Life turns low HP into a damage window, and its cost also lands on HP. Armory Ledger only pays out once the weapon set is complete; otherwise it is a pure loss. The cost is not a flat nerf but a change in what the player has to weigh.
  • Principle 2Separate team-wide from personal effectsPlague Pact applies the same status effect to the whole team, while Dice of Fate and Armory Ledger are drawn per player, so teammates can differ and complement each other. Every law states whether it applies to the team or to each player.
  • Principle 3Tune by player countValues are set separately for 1, 2 and 3 players. Shoulder to Shoulder only appears with 2–3 players, and Shared Cup switches to a self-only version in solo runs. Laws lock to the player count at departure, so a mid-run disconnect does not trigger a recalculation.
  • Principle 4Only combine effects that already existAll 12 laws are built from the effects of existing relics and items, with no new animations or models, which lowers the risk of co-op desyncs and crashes.

04

Deep of Night

Deep of Night also has Night Laws, the new Nightlords and the Lake of Rot, and the base game's Everdark Sovereigns share the random pool with the new Nightlords.

The game identifies a target only by a base Nightlord ID plus an Everdark flag; there is no third kind of identity, so the new Nightlords had to borrow the Everdark Sovereign slots. The first version therefore pushed the Everdark Sovereigns out of Deep of Night.

In the revised version, each run decides who takes that slot. The 7 base-game Everdark Sovereigns were added back to the Deep of Night draw table with their original weights (none at Depth 1, more common the deeper you go). When a base-game Sovereign is drawn, a native plugin swaps the Night 3 arena files back to vanilla and temporarily restores the menu name and icon in memory. In co-op, every machine derives the result independently from the run's layout after landing, so all players agree.

Deep of Night depth can be set directly in the config file (1–5), so players do not have to climb from Depth 1 again, and the mode no longer depends on an unlock flag in the save.

05

Player feedback after release

Within three days of the beta, players reported six kinds of problems. Each one was traced to its root cause before being fixed, and the fixes shipped in the 11.02 patch and the 11.03 full release.

Player reportRoot causeFix
In co-op, Night Laws only applied to the hostNetwork state written by the host does not sync to Seamless Co-op clientsEvery machine derives the same law independently from the run's map layout, with no reliance on network sync
Teammates did not get the relic effect “attack up after defeating night invaders”The base game only counted on the host's sideEach machine now records invader deaths and counts them itself

All fixes above were verified in solo play; co-op behavior is still being confirmed by testers.
