Hearthstone Battlegrounds
Economy & Match Pacing

Battlegrounds has no card collection: 8 players share one minion pool, and the result depends almost entirely on in-match resource decisions. This teardown looks only at a single match: how Gold comes in and is spent, how the minion pool limits choice, and how the gap between strong and weak boards opens up and is then reined in. Based on Season 14, October 2026.

01

Summary

The core tension: 8 players with identical income share one minion pool, yet every match has to play out differently. The gap between strong and weak has to open up, but not decide the match too early.

  • Finding 1Income and prices are fixed; differences come only from spendingGold rises by 1 per turn to a cap of 10 on turn 8, and every minion costs 3. Each turn comes down to levelling the Tavern or buying power, with health as the stake.
  • Finding 2Scarcity comes from copy counts, not odds tablesA Tier 6 card has only 7 copies in the whole lobby, and one triple takes 43% of them. The shared pool also creates a mind game around contested tribes and reading opponents.
  • Finding 3Snowballing comes from the board, and it has brakesThere is no interest or win-streak bonus, and there are damage caps, copy limits and Discovers only one tier up; hero strength is balanced by converting armor into health.
  • Finding 4Add one seasonal mechanic, remove oneEach season's new mechanic is an extra choice outside the shop. The underlying economy stays fixed, and only one or two extra systems are live at a time.

02

Monetization & experience

Payment only buys choice. The season pass widens the starting hero pick from 2 to 4, plus cosmetics. It sells no in-match stats, but the number of hero options still affects ranked play.

The experience centers on challenge and exploration, serving mainly achievers and explorers. Since only the top 4 gain rating, 5th place hurts the most and makes players most eager to queue again. Social play is this mode's weak spot.

Compared with Naruto Mobile: Naruto's progression accumulates over months and years, and payment can buy progress directly; Battlegrounds compresses progression into a single match and payment barely touches in-match stats, so the system design lives in the economy curve, the scarcity of shared resources and the pace at which strong and weak boards diverge.

03

Improvements

PriorityProposal
①A three-level hint in the shop for remaining copies, with an A/B test plan
②A tutorial match for each season's new mechanic at season start
③A uniform number of hero options in ranked
④Mid-game catch-up for players who fall behind

I also modeled making Tavern upgrade costs linear and concluded it should not be changed.
